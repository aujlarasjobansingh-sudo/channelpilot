# YouTube Growth Playbook

## How discovery actually works
YouTube's job is to keep viewers watching and satisfied. Every recommendation answers one question:
"will this person click, watch, and feel it was worth it?" Two numbers decide most of it:

1. **Packaging** — title + thumbnail. Gets the click. No click, no views, no matter how good the video is.
2. **Satisfaction** — did they watch and feel good after? Measured through watch time, retention,
   likes, comments, shares, and survey responses. High satisfaction = more recommendations.

Traffic sources that matter: **Browse features** (home feed), **Suggested** (next to/below videos),
**Search** (evergreen topics), **Shorts shelf** (fast discovery, weak subscribers per view).

## The three levers, in order
1. **Packaging.** Spend as long on title + thumbnail as on editing. One clear idea per video.
   Curiosity gap + specific payoff: "I Built X in 24 Hours" beats "My Project Vlog #12".
2. **Retention.** First 30 seconds decide everything: state the payoff, skip the long intro.
   Re-hook every 60–90 seconds (open loops, new visuals, pattern interrupts). Cut ruthlessly.
3. **Consistency.** A sustainable cadence beats bursts. Series and formats compound because
   viewers know what they're getting and the algorithm knows who to show it to.

## Shorts strategy
- Hook in the first 1 second. Loop-friendly endings perform well.
- Use Shorts for discovery, then funnel: pin a comment linking the related long video,
  use "related video" linking on Shorts.
- Don't spam 5 Shorts a day hoping one pops — 3–5 quality Shorts a week beats volume.

## Search (evergreen) strategy
- Target what people actually search: "how to X", "X vs Y", "best X for Y".
- Put the keyword in the title and the first 2 lines of the description.
- Evergreen videos earn for years; trend videos spike and die. A healthy channel has both.

## Community
- Pin a comment on every video (question + CTA). Reply to early comments fast.
- Community posts/polls between uploads keep the channel warm.
- Collaborations and guest appearances borrow trust — pick channels with overlapping audiences.

## Realistic timelines
- Expect 50–100 videos before you find your format. Judge in batches, not per video.
- Compare each video against YOUR channel average, not against MrBeast.
- One breakout video doesn't mean the channel changed; watch whether the NEXT videos hold.

## Common mistakes
- Topic-hopping: confuses the audience and the algorithm. Niche down until growth is stable.
- Clickbait mismatch: title promises X, video delivers Y → low retention → burial.
- Ignoring analytics: if you don't know your CTR and retention, you're guessing.
- Buying subs/views: kills reach, risks termination. Never.
- Quitting at video 12: almost every growing channel looked dead at video 12.
