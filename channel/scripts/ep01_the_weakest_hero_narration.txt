What Happens If You Become the Weakest Hero Alive?

What happens if you wake up in a world where giant monsters attack every single week where heroes are ranked like celebrities, and every eighteen-year-old gets a number that decides their whole life and your number is zero.

Everyone calls you the weakest hero alive. Your own rank card says it. The news says it. Even the kids on your street say it.

But there's one thing nobody in this city knows about you.

And by the end of today, the strongest hero in the world is going to find out the hard way.

Before we start, if you like stories where you are the main character, hit subscribe. Every week, we drop you into a brand-new world.

Now… let's go.

In this world, monsters don't hide in movies. They come out of the ground.

Nobody knows exactly where they come from. Scientists call them breach creatures. Everyone else just calls them monsters. Some are the size of a dog. Some are the size of a building. And every single one gets a threat level.

Ember level: dangerous, but a couple of police officers can handle it. Storm level: it can flatten a street. You need a hero. Quake level: it can wipe out a whole district. Eclipse level: it can end a city. And Extinction level… nobody likes to talk about Extinction level. The last one was forty years ago, and the map still has a hole where a city used to be.

So the world needed protection. And that's how the Bureau was born.

The Bureau of Registered Heroes runs everything. It trains heroes, pays heroes, ranks heroes, and, most importantly, sells heroes. Hero merchandise. Hero TV shows. Hero energy drinks. In Meridian City, heroes aren't just protectors. They're the biggest celebrities on the planet.

And every hero has a rank.

Iron is the bottom. Iron heroes rescue cats and direct traffic. Bronze and Silver handle the everyday monsters. Gold heroes get the billboards. And Crown… there are only seven Crown heroes in the entire world. They're treated like gods.

So how does the Bureau decide who becomes what?

Simple. On your eighteenth birthday, you walk into a Bureau center and put your hand on a machine called the Scanner. It reads the energy inside your body and gives you a number between zero and one thousand. Your Spark Score.

Score above a hundred? Congratulations, you're a hero. Score above five hundred? You're going to be famous. Score above nine hundred? You'll be Crown rank before you're twenty-five.

That number is your whole future. Your job. Your money. How people look at you on the street.

Which brings us to you.

Four years ago, on your eighteenth birthday, you walked into the Bureau center with your mom. You were wearing your favourite red t-shirt, the one with the little white lightning bolt. You'd drawn that bolt yourself when you were nine, because you wanted to be a hero so badly it hurt.

You put your hand on the Scanner.

The lights started to climb… and then stopped. Right at the bottom.

Zero.

Not five. Not one. Zero. The technician actually laughed. He said in twenty years he'd never seen a real zero. He even took a photo of the screen.

By the next morning, that photo was everywhere. "The Zero Kid." "The Most Powerless Human Ever Scanned." People made memes. Someone made a song.

The Bureau still gave you a hero license, because technically anyone who applies gets one. But they made you Iron rank, number 4,812. The very last hero in the entire city.

So for four years, that's what you've been. You help old ladies cross the street. You get cats out of trees. Once, you spent six hours guarding a parking lot because a Gold hero was filming a commercial there.

And every day, someone reminds you what you are.

"Hey, Zero, saved any cats today?" "Careful, Zero, don't hurt yourself."

You smile, you wave, you keep walking.

Because here's the thing. Deep down, there's a feeling you've never told anyone about. Every time someone laughs at you, your hands get warm. Like something inside you is… waking up.

You always thought it was just anger.

You were wrong.

It's a Tuesday. You're at Central Station, carrying groceries for Mrs. Delgado from the fourth floor, when the ground starts shaking.

Then the floor of Platform 6 explodes.

A Storm-level monster. The kind that needs a Silver hero at least. Alarms are screaming. People are running in every direction. Somewhere a kid is crying for her dad.

And the nearest registered hero is… you.

You check your phone. The Bureau app says: "Silver hero dispatched. Estimated arrival: 9 minutes."

Nine minutes. In nine minutes, this thing can tear the whole station apart.

The monster turns. It sees the little girl, standing alone by a pillar. It opens a mouth full of teeth the size of kitchen knives.

You don't think. You just run.

You jump in front of the girl. The monster's claw comes down. And as you raise your arm to protect your face, you hear someone behind you on the platform shout:

"Get out of the way, Zero! You're useless!"

And your hands get warm.

No. Not warm.

They burn.

When the dust settles, the monster is gone. Not knocked back. Not defeated. Gone. There's a hole in the station roof shaped like a monster, and through it you can see blue sky.

You look at your fist. It's not even scratched.

The little girl is staring at you. Everyone is staring at you.

And then, through the smoke, the Silver hero finally arrives, lands in a superhero pose, looks at the empty platform, and says:

"…Did I miss something?"

Here's the funny part.

The security cameras were destroyed by the shockwave. The station was full of dust. And when reporters asked the witnesses what happened, half of them said "a hero in red punched it", and the other half said "there's no way it was the Zero kid."

By the evening news, the official story was this: "Silver hero arrives just in time to scare off a Storm-level monster."

You sit on your bed that night, staring at your hand.

You punched a Storm-level monster into the sky. With a Spark Score of zero.

So either the Scanner is broken… or you're something the Scanner was never built to read.

You need to know which one.

You spend the next two weeks running experiments.

Experiment one: you try to punch a tree in the park. Nothing. Your knuckles hurt for three days.

Experiment two: you try to lift a car. The car does not move. A man walks past and says, "Bro, what are you doing?"

Experiment three: you go home angry and frustrated. Your little cousin laughs and says, "You're so weak, you couldn't even beat me in arm wrestling."

You challenge her. And the moment your hands touch, there's that warmth again.

Your cousin screams. Your aunt screams. The table is very, very broken.

And that's when you finally understand.

Your power doesn't come from inside you.

It comes from them.

Every time someone believes you're weak, every time someone laughs at you, doubts you, looks down on you, you get stronger. One person's doubt makes you as strong as a heavyweight boxer. A whole crowd's doubt?

That was a crowd.

Think about what that means. For four years, an entire city has been calling you the weakest hero alive. Millions of people. Every meme. Every joke. Every comment.

And every single one of them has been charging you up like a battery.

The Scanner reads zero because the Scanner only measures power that's yours. And yours isn't yours. It's borrowed. From everyone who's ever looked down on you.

Which means the weaker the world thinks you are… the stronger you become.

You should feel happy. Instead, a cold thought hits you.

What happens if people stop laughing?

You don't tell anyone. But somebody figures it out anyway.

Her name is Mira Okafor. Silver rank. Spark Score 540. She builds her own gadgets, flies her own drones, and she was the second hero to arrive at the train station that day.

And unlike everyone else, she looked at the hole in the roof.

"Monsters don't go up," she tells you, sitting on your fire escape with a tablet full of data. "They go sideways, they go down. That one went up. Like something hit it from below. Something with about four hundred times the force of my strongest punch."

She turns the tablet around. It's a blurry frame from her drone camera. A red t-shirt. A white bolt.

"So," she says. "Zero. Want to tell me what you are?"

You could lie. But Mira is the first person in four years who's looked at you like you might be more.

So you tell her everything.

She doesn't laugh. She just goes very quiet. Then she says something you'll remember for a long time:

"You know the Bureau would never let someone like you exist, right?"

You ask her what she means.

"Think about it. The Bureau makes billions selling heroes. Rankings, toys, TV deals. Their whole system says the number decides who's strong. And you're a zero who can do what a Crown can do. If people found out, the whole system collapses."

"So keep your head down," she says. "And let them keep laughing."

Over the next month, the monster attacks get worse.

Normally the city gets one or two Storm-level monsters a month. Now it's three a week. Then a Quake-level, the first one in six years, right in the middle of the shopping district.

You and Mira start showing up to every attack. She flies in with her drones. You "help with evacuation." And when nobody is watching, you end the fight with one punch.

But Mira keeps staring at her data. Something doesn't add up.

"Look at the locations," she says one night, spreading maps across your floor.

You look. Every single attack happened within two blocks of a Gold or Crown hero who was already there. Filming a commercial. Doing a fan event. Shooting a TV special.

"Monsters don't follow celebrities," Mira says. "But these ones do. Every attack is perfectly timed, so the right hero is there, the cameras are rolling, and the city gets a hero moment."

You both say the name at the same time.

Aurex.

Aurex is the number one hero in the world. Crown rank. Spark Score 998. They call him the Golden Wall, because no monster has ever gotten past him.

He's also the face of the Bureau. Every billboard, every cereal box, every TV ad. When people imagine the word hero, they imagine Aurex.

And according to Mira's map, Aurex has been at the scene of nineteen of the last twenty attacks.

"Maybe he's just fast," you say.

"Or maybe," Mira says, "he knows where they're going to appear before they appear."

That night, Mira hacks into the Bureau's old monster-research files. Most of it is locked. But one folder isn't, because whoever hid it never imagined a Silver-rank nobody would go looking.

The folder is called "Project Lantern."

Inside are photos. Breach points, the holes monsters come out of, but these ones have metal frames built around them. Machines. Bureau logos.

The Bureau isn't just fighting the monsters.

It's opening the doors for them.

You have proof. You have Mira. And you have the strongest punch in the city.

You should be unstoppable.

But then something goes wrong. Something you never saw coming.

Remember the little girl from the train station? Her dad filmed part of it from the stairs. A shaky, blurry, twelve-second clip. And for the first time, you can see it clearly: a red t-shirt, a white bolt, and a monster flying through a roof.

The internet goes insane.

"WAS IT THE ZERO KID?!" "The Zero is secretly the strongest hero??" "I always knew he was hiding something."

People start cheering for you. Believing in you. Kids draw your bolt on their notebooks. Strangers stop you on the street to shake your hand.

And every single time they do… you feel it.

You're getting weaker.

Every fan, every cheer, every "I believe in you" drains the battery. A week after the video goes viral, you try to punch a parking meter, and you hurt your hand.

For the first time in your life, the whole city loves you.

And it's killing your power.

Then Aurex makes his move.

At 6:14 PM on a Friday, the biggest breach in forty years opens in the middle of Meridian City. And out of it climbs something that makes every monster before it look like a pet.

Eclipse level. The Bureau names it Gorrak. It's taller than the buildings around it. Its roar knocks people off their feet three blocks away.

Every phone in the city buzzes with the same alert: "EVACUATE. ECLIPSE-LEVEL THREAT. CROWN HEROES DISPATCHED."

And then Aurex appears. Floating above the city, golden armour shining, every news camera pointed at him. This is the moment. The biggest hero moment in history.

But instead of fighting… he talks.

"People of Meridian City," he says. "Before I save you tonight, you deserve the truth. There's a lie going around about a so-called hero. The Zero. A boy with no power, no rank and no talent, who's been stealing credit from real heroes."

"He's a fraud. A nobody. The weakest hero ever scanned. And tonight, while he hides, I'll show you what a real hero looks like."

Mira grabs your arm. "He knows," she whispers. "He found the video. He's trying to make the city turn on you before you can tell them about Project Lantern."

But you're not looking at her. You're looking at your hands.

Because all across the city, people are staring at their screens. Millions of them. And millions of them are starting to think the same thing:

Maybe Aurex is right. Maybe the Zero really is nothing.

Aurex doesn't know your rule.

He just told the entire city you're the weakest person alive.

He just gave you the biggest charge of your life.

Gorrak sees you. All three eyes lock onto the tiny figure in the red t-shirt.

Aurex sees you too. For a second, his smile slips. "Go home, Zero," he says into the cameras. "You're embarrassing yourself."

Thank you, you think.

Gorrak roars and brings one giant claw down, straight at you.

You don't dodge. You don't block.

You punch.

The shockwave rolls down the avenue. Every car alarm in the city goes off at once. And Gorrak, an Eclipse-level monster that was supposed to destroy Meridian City, is a fading purple dot in the sky.

And it doesn't stop there.

The force of the punch rips the breach wide open, and underneath it everyone sees what's buried there: a Bureau machine, glowing purple, with the Bureau logo stamped on the side.

Every news camera in the city is pointed right at it. Live. In 4K.

Mira's drones are already uploading the Project Lantern files to every screen in Meridian City.

The Golden Wall just watched his whole lie fall apart.

By morning, it's over. Aurex is arrested. The Bureau's leaders are under investigation. Project Lantern is shut down, and for the first time in years, Meridian City goes a full month without a single monster attack.

And the city knows the truth now. The Zero is real.

The new Bureau director offers you everything. Crown rank. Number one. Your own billboard. Your face on every cereal box in the country.

"The people love you," she says. "They want you to be their hero."

And you think about it. Really think about it. Everything you ever wanted when you drew that little white bolt at nine years old.

But you know your rule.

If they love you, you get weaker. If you become their Crown hero, the next time an Eclipse-level monster crawls out of the ground, you'll be standing there with nothing.

So you do the only thing that makes sense.

You say no.

You ask to stay Iron rank, number 4,812. Last place. The Zero.

The director thinks you're crazy. The news calls it "the strangest decision in hero history." Half the city calls you humble. The other half calls you an idiot.

That second half is exactly what you need.

So now, here you are. Back to rescuing cats. Back to guarding parking lots. Back to kids on your street shouting "Hey Zero, saved any cats today?"

And every time they do, you smile, you wave, and you keep walking.

Because they think they're laughing at the weakest hero alive.

They have no idea they're protecting the city.

"By the way," Mira says. "I ran your Scanner data again last night. With my own software."

You ask what it says.

She turns the tablet around.

"It's not zero anymore," she says quietly. "And it's not stopping."

So that's what happens if you become the weakest hero alive.
