When the dust settles, the monster is gone. Not knocked back. Not defeated. Gone. There's a hole in the station roof shaped like a monster, and through it you can see blue sky.

You look at your fist. It's not even scratched.

The little girl is staring at you. Everyone is staring at you.

And then, through the smoke, the Silver hero finally arrives, lands in a superhero pose, looks at the empty platform, and says:

"…Did I miss something?"

Here's the funny part.

The security cameras were destroyed by the shockwave. The station was full of dust. And when reporters asked the witnesses what happened, half of them said "a hero in red punched it", and the other half said "there's no way it was the Zero kid."

By the evening news, the official story was this: "Silver hero arrives just in time to scare off a Storm-level monster."

You sit on your bed that night, staring at your hand.

You punched a Storm-level monster into the sky. With a Spark Score of zero.

So either the Scanner is broken… or you're something the Scanner was never built to read.

You need to know which one.

You spend the next two weeks running experiments.

Experiment one: you try to punch a tree in the park. Nothing. Your knuckles hurt for three days.

Experiment two: you try to lift a car. The car does not move. A man walks past and says, "Bro, what are you doing?"

Experiment three: you go home angry and frustrated. Your little cousin laughs and says, "You're so weak, you couldn't even beat me in arm wrestling."

You challenge her. And the moment your hands touch, there's that warmth again.

Your cousin screams. Your aunt screams. The table is very, very broken.

And that's when you finally understand.

Your power doesn't come from inside you.

It comes from them.

Every time someone believes you're weak, every time someone laughs at you, doubts you, looks down on you, you get stronger. One person's doubt makes you as strong as a heavyweight boxer. A whole crowd's doubt?

That was a crowd.

Think about what that means. For four years, an entire city has been calling you the weakest hero alive. Millions of people. Every meme. Every joke. Every comment.

And every single one of them has been charging you up like a battery.

The Scanner reads zero because the Scanner only measures power that's yours. And yours isn't yours. It's borrowed. From everyone who's ever looked down on you.

Which means the weaker the world thinks you are… the stronger you become.

You should feel happy. Instead, a cold thought hits you.

What happens if people stop laughing?

You don't tell anyone. But somebody figures it out anyway.

Her name is Mira Okafor. Silver rank. Spark Score 540. She builds her own gadgets, flies her own drones, and she was the second hero to arrive at the train station that day.

And unlike everyone else, she looked at the hole in the roof.

"Monsters don't go up," she tells you, sitting on your fire escape with a tablet full of data. "They go sideways, they go down. That one went up. Like something hit it from below. Something with about four hundred times the force of my strongest punch."

She turns the tablet around. It's a blurry frame from her drone camera. A red t-shirt. A white bolt.

"So," she says. "Zero. Want to tell me what you are?"

You could lie. But Mira is the first person in four years who's looked at you like you might be more.

So you tell her everything.

She doesn't laugh. She just goes very quiet. Then she says something you'll remember for a long time:

"You know the Bureau would never let someone like you exist, right?"

You ask her what she means.

"Think about it. The Bureau makes billions selling heroes. Rankings, toys, TV deals. Their whole system says the number decides who's strong. And you're a zero who can do what a Crown can do. If people found out, the whole system collapses."

"So keep your head down," she says. "And let them keep laughing."

Over the next month, the monster attacks get worse.

Normally the city gets one or two Storm-level monsters a month. Now it's three a week. Then a Quake-level, the first one in six years, right in the middle of the shopping district.

You and Mira start showing up to every attack. She flies in with her drones. You "help with evacuation." And when nobody is watching, you end the fight with one punch.

But Mira keeps staring at her data. Something doesn't add up.

"Look at the locations," she says one night, spreading maps across your floor.

You look. Every single attack happened within two blocks of a Gold or Crown hero who was already there. Filming a commercial. Doing a fan event. Shooting a TV special.

"Monsters don't follow celebrities," Mira says. "But these ones do. Every attack is perfectly timed, so the right hero is there, the cameras are rolling, and the city gets a hero moment."

You both say the name at the same time.

Aurex.
